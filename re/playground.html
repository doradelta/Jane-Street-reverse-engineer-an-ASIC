Probe the silicon

Everything on this bench runs on the actual netlist I pulled out of puzzle.gds, compiled to JavaScript. Each time you touch the grid the page reruns all 615 gates and 92 flip flops through the whole 142 cycle protocol and shows you what the chip decided.

The counts turn teal when a line holds exactly two stars, and stars turn red when they touch. Those hints come from the page, not from the chip; success is decided by the simulated circuit alone. Knowing the rules is not enough either, since the chip only accepts one exact grid.

What the chip is

Underneath it all, the chip validates one specific Star Battle grid, 11×11.

You clock in 121 bits, one row at a time, with enable high, then drop enable. The grid it accepts is a proper Star Battle solution: two stars in every row, two in every column, and no two stars touching, diagonals included. Those rules alone still admit plenty of grids though. The chip also hardwires which puzzle it is checking, so every other rule abiding grid gets rejected too. z3 confirms the whole picture: exactly one input clears the full circuit.
